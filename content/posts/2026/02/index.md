# Japan trip

So, a dream finally came true after years and years of wondering if I would ever get here. It's currently the first of July, and I am sitting in my hotel, [Hotel Onyado Nono Kyoto Shichijo Natural Hot Spring](https://onyado-nono-kyoto-shichijo-natural-hot-spring.hotels-in-kyoto.com/en/), in Kyoto as I start writing this blog.

## Day 1 - 24th June

I arrived at Tokyo Haneda Airport around 11:00 PM after a 7-hour flight from Malaysia. The Tokyo night view from above was f***ing breathtaking. Not gonna lie, I shed a few tears when I saw that view.

My itinerary looked like this: 
* Arrive in Tokyo -> Take domestic flight to Osaka -> Stay in Osaka -> Head to Kyoto for 2 days -> Return to Osaka -> Head back to Tokyo for the rest of the trip.

### Layover at tokyo Haneda Airport
I landed at Terminal 2 at 11:00 PM, and my connection to Osaka wasn't until 8:00 AM the next morning. Back when I was planning, I thought, *"Hey, I have a 9-hour transit! I can just walk around and collect some [Eki Stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eki_stamp)."* Boy, was I wrong! 

Basically, almost every Information Service desk—whether in the airport or railway stations has a unique stamp you can collect, but I completely forgot to check if they closed at night. I ended up spending the next 2 hours just wandering around a mostly quiet Terminal 2.

I got my personalized Suica card for the metro. Of course, I had to get my name printed on it!

Around midnight, a security guard came up to inform me that Terminal 2 was closing. (*Wait, what?! I didn't know airport terminals closed! Where the hell am I supposed to go? My whole plan was to crash at the terminal!*). 

He told me I needed to transfer over to Terminal 3. Apparently, Haneda shuts down Terminals 1 and 2 around midnight, so everyone has to head to Terminal 3 to rest until morning. So I hopped on the free airport interterminal shuttle bus and made my way over.

## Day 2 -  25th June Osaka

At 6:00 AM, I waited until the Information Service desk opened and finally got my very first Eki Stamp! There were actually a few more on the upper floors, but since I was worried about missing my flight, I gave up on those and went straight to the free shuttle bus back to Terminal 2.

  <img src="./images/07.png" alt="image01" style="width: 60%; height: auto;">

Terminal 2 was absolutely bombarded with people as I entered, a huge contrast to last night, when it was completely empty! I checked in and headed straight to my gate. I was so excited to see Mount Fuji from the plane and had specifically booked a window seat just for the view. But guess what? Two nasty typhoons were hitting Japan, and as "lucky" as I am, it was completely cloudy so I didn't get to see it at all!

### Apa hotel & Exploring

I was starving at this point. As I walked down the street, I spotted four Japanese salarymen heading into a nearby building. I decided to follow their lead and went into the exact same restaurant they entered!

I sat down and ordered a Conger Eel (Anago) Bento Set, and it was *so* good! It came with cold soba, eel over rice (the sauce was amazing!), hot miso soup, and a side broth to mix with the soba.

## Day 10 - First Day in Shinjuku, Tokyo (Pre-Ado Concert)

Finally, the day I’ve been waiting for! 

I woke up around 8:00 AM, and since checkout was at 10:00 AM, I got straight to organizing my luggage. Carrying a massive suitcase with me all the way from Osaka to Tokyo would have been a complete pain in the back. Thankfully, my hotel offered luggage forwarding! I went down to the lobby around 9:30 AM, and the staff member there was super friendly and helpful. I provided him with my next hotel details, and the total cost for forwarding was around ¥2,000.

To get from Osaka to Tokyo, I took the Shinkansen (the bullet train). I had already booked my ticket prior to arriving in Japan. While waiting on the platform, I noticed several other fans wearing Ado merch!

Ado was actually holding a two-day concert at [Nissan Stadium](https://www.nissan-stadium.jp/) in Yokohama, and I was lucky enough to secure a ticket for the second day. I initially thought I had bought a ticket for the first day, but after triple-checking my confirmation email later on, I realized it was actually for day two. Anyway... I really wanted to go to the first day's show as well, but Ticketboard was only selling day two tickets when I checked—which sucked because I already had one for that day!

Anyway, I hopped on the Shinkansen. If you remember from earlier when I flew into Japan, I booked a seat specifically to catch a view of Mount Fuji, but couldn't because of the typhoon. Well, I made sure to book the Mount Fuji side for this train ride, too. BUT GUESS WHAT?? MOUNT FUJI WAS SHY AGAIN! I didn't get to see it fully just a tiny bit of the tip peaking out through the clouds.

<figure>
  <img src="./images/23.png" alt="image01" style="width: 60%; height: auto;">
</figure>

I got off the Shinkansen at Tokyo Station and transferred to the subway to head to Shinjuku. Surprisingly, even though I had fully prepared myself to get completely lost inside Shinjuku Station, navigating to the exit was actually pretty straightforward! 

By the time I made it out, it was already around 3:00 PM (15:00), so I went straight to my hotel to check in. I stayed at the [Shinjuku Prince Hotel](https://www.princehotels.com/shinjuku/), which was included in the official JTB travel package I booked to secure my concert ticket.

I stopped by the JTB  desk in the lobby to pick up my concert ticket and to my surprise, they also handed me a bundle of bonus concert goodies! Major kudos to them!

## Day 11 - Day of the Concert

Ahhhhh!!! The day is finally here!

Yesterday was Day 1 of the concert, and I successfully managed to avoid every single setlist spoiler online. However, I found out that fans both yesterday and today were already queuing up as early as 6:00 AM. What are you guys doing?! The concert doesn't even start until 6:00 PM (18:00)! But then again... what do I know? This was my very first time attending a concert in my entire life! I also saw that Adidas was selling a special FIFA x Ado collaboration jersey. I wanted to get one so badly, but it sold out almost instantly.

I left my hotel around noon to head over to Yokohama, where the concert was being held and boy, I did NOT expect to get lost as many times as I did. I took the train towards Yokohama Station, intending to transfer to the line heading toward Shin-Yokohama Station near Nissan Stadium. But somehow, I don't even know how I managed it, I completely messed up the transfer and ended up in places I'd never even heard of!

#### Women's Doubles

Women's Doubles featured the Korean pair [Kim Hye Jeong](https://bwfbadminton.com/player/83419/kim-hye-jeong) and [Kong Hee Yong](https://bwfbadminton.com/player/71391/kong-hee-yong) vs. the Chinese pair [Zhang Shu Xian](https://bwfbadminton.com/player/85914/zhang-shu-xian) and [Jia Yi Fan](https://bwfbadminton.com/player/65144/jia-yi-fan).

This match went to a deciding third set, and honest to God, we were treated to absolute peak badminton.

The third set went deep into extra points: 20-20, 21-20, 21-21... all the way up to 26-26, 27-27!

The photographers courtside added so much unintentional comedy to the moment. Every time the Korean pair held match point, all the photographers would shuffle back and forth (*aller-retour*) to focus on them, and the second the Chinese pair pulled ahead, they’d all hurry back to the other side to cover them instead. Watching them switch back and forth on every single point was hilarious!

The match finally reached the ultimate sudden-death point at 29-29! On the final rally, Zhang Shu Xian hit a shot over the baseline; she initially thought she won the match, while the Korean pair immediately celebrated, knowing it was out. Absolute drama until the very last second! China called for a Hawk-Eye challenge, but the review confirmed the shuttle was out, handing the victory to the Korean pair.

What a match! If you want to watch the full replay, [here's the link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HycCF1t6kxg).
